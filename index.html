Human Era vs Minions

Before-and-after productivity for Deliveri, measured across all 37 company GitHub repositories, Jira (DLC and GEN) and GitHub Actions. Written for an audience of engineering leaders, so the comparison favors fairness over a dramatic number.

Dates used, and why there are three periods

Human era2024-04-26 → 2026-05-29

109 weeks. The first commit in any company repo is 2024-04-26. Nothing exists between 1 Jan 2024 and then, so per-week averages start at the first commit. The era ends with the last engineer's final commit on 2026-05-29.

Transition2026-05-30 → 2026-08-26

12.6 weeks. No engineers. The two directors worked hands-on, and from 2026-06-10 drove Claude Code directly, without the Minions harness. This period is reported separately and kept out of the main comparison.

Minions era2026-08-26 10:32 ET → today

6.2 weeks. Starts at commit 8fafb458d, "install the Minions framework" (DLC-611). The first Minions run and the first autopilot batch followed within a day.

Why not June 2026? June is when Claude Code started: the first agent co-authored commits by the directors are dated 2026-06-10. The Minions harness itself was installed on 2026-08-26. If the human era ran up to the Minions cutover, it would include 12 weeks of AI-agent work, and the "human" baseline would no longer be human. The literal version is still given at the end for completeness.

Read before you post

  • Most of the jump happened before Minions. Agent-assisted work without the harness (the transition) already completed about 22 tickets a week. Minions raised that to about 29 tickets a week, with GEN adding a second product line. "AI agents vs our old team" and "Minions vs plain Claude Code" are different claims.
  • The human team shrank to one engineer for its last year. From May 2025 to May 2026, one engineer made 97% of the commits. Two-year averages mix a 4 to 6 person period with a solo period.
  • The units changed. The human team committed straight to develop and used PRs mainly to promote releases (131 of 253 merged PRs). Minions opens one PR per ticket and squashes on merge. Commits and PRs are not like-for-like.
  • "Complete" means UAT or Done: code finished and deployed to dev, for both DLC and GEN. Production releases are batched by choice and are not used as a productivity measure.
  • The Minions era is 6 weeks long and includes a planned build-out of the internal Genesis admin app. Six weeks against two years is a short sample.

Three comparisons that hold up

Testing
56→7,673

Automated test cases: 56 written in two years of the human era, 7,673 added in the first six weeks on Minions.

Static count of test declarations across every repo at the human era's end, with duplicate files removed, against the monorepo's own count. Quote both absolute numbers, not a multiplier. A ratio this large reads as a gimmick, and the honest story is that the old codebase had almost no tests.

Tickets completed
2.0→29.4

Tickets completed per week across the customer app and the Genesis admin app: 15× the human-era rate. Feature and capability tickets alone rose from 1.6 to 22.5 a week (14.5×).

Complete means the code is done and deployed to dev (UAT or Done), from Jira status history. Ticket size is the open question, because Minions plans work as smaller slices. Say so.

Build time per ticket
15.2 h→3.3 h

Median time from a ticket being started to the code being complete, 4.6× faster.

Both eras used the same Jira "In Progress" status, so this is a like-for-like measure (131 human tickets, 134 Minions tickets). It covers only time in progress. Minions also works several tickets in parallel.

Also defensible: source code added per week went from 2,268 to 15,025 lines (6.6×), excluding tests, lockfiles, generated files, migrations, build output and vendored code. The counting method favors the human era, because their lines are summed commit by commit while Minions' come from squash merges.

Avoid quoting: commit counts (the squash habit cuts them on trunk), PR multipliers (the human team used PRs for releases), and the 2,400× test ratio.

  • Shopify store connection and order import

Minions era
  • Genesis internal admin app: sign-in, order page, orders list, staff actions, USPS pickups
  • Courier Estimated Delivery Date and Delivered On on orders

  • Sign-up rework: phone entry with dial codes, business address step

  • Permanent reference IDs, per-label records, time-zone-safe timestamps
  • Batched production release process with a release queue

Per engineer EST: era totals divided by engineer-weeks. The low end assumes every person active in a month worked all of that month (257 engineer-weeks). The high end counts only weeks in which a person committed (196). Real effort is somewhere in between, because part-time and contract status isn't recorded. Ticket rates use the 117 commit-weeks inside the Jira period at the high end and about 153 at the low end (est.).

Solo-engineer year EXACT: 2025-05-01 to 2026-05-29 (56.3 weeks). One engineer made 97% of the 681 commits, with a short-term contributor and a director making the rest. This is the cleanest one-engineer baseline, since it involves no team coordination. It also covers the period when Jira was barely used, which lowers its ticket rate.

The Minions column isn't zero people. One director spends an estimated 22 to 47 hours a fortnight directing agents, about 35 at the midpoint (from the previous audit's session analysis). Per director-hour, the ratios are smaller than per engineer-week.

Your literal definition, for completeness

If the human era runs from 2024 right up to the Minions cutover (2026-08-26), it absorbs the transition. That baseline becomes 121.8 weeks with 437 completed tickets, 529 merged PRs, 3,835 commits, 2,405 test cases and +359,513 source lines. The multipliers shrink to about 6.2× completed tickets (on Jira weeks), 8.5× PRs merged, 3.4× commits, 5.1× source lines and 63× tests per week EST. I don't recommend this framing, because 12 of those weeks were already agent-driven.

Sources

Git: all 37 repositories in the ondeliveri GitHub organization, cloned including every refs/pull/* head so deleted PR branches count (6,513 commits). The monorepo imported the old web app and backend histories, so 2,006 duplicate commits were removed by matching author, subject, date and diff size. "Trunk" means commits reachable from main, master, prod, develop, dev or qa.

Accounts excluded: github-actions[bot] (4 commits, prod image-tag syncs). No dependabot or other bot accounts exist. The Claude Code git identity (8 commits) is treated as an agent, not a human. Known aliases of the same person were merged before counting people. No individual is named or ranked here.

GitHub: 780 PRs across all repos, and Actions history for every deploy and promote workflow. Jira: 823 DLC and GEN issues with full status history. A ticket counts as complete at its first move to UAT, or at Done if it skipped UAT. Epics and Won't Do tickets are excluded.
